A few months ago I started building a gamified task system, and it began with my kids — but the more I thought about it, the more I realized this isn't really a kids problem at all.

We tried Habitica with them first — the gamified task tracker with XP, loot, monster battles. Good idea, weak execution: why brush your teeth if the reward is a pixelated sword that does nothing? The motivation never connected to anything real.

But here's the thing — adults have the exact same problem. We're not scrolling Reels or binge-watching a series because we lack willpower. The modern attention economy has gotten scarily good at hooking into our dopamine circuitry, almost like a drug, and it works on grown adults just as well as it works on ten-year-olds. That's not going away, and fighting it head-on rarely works, at any age.

But there's another way to use the same wiring. It's basically classical conditioning — the same idea behind Karen Pryor's *Don't Shoot the Dog*. If you do the dishes, or pull weeds, or clear a backlog at work, specifically to earn something you already want — say, an hour of guilt-free Netflix — you build a real behavioral loop. And here's the interesting part: once that loop is established, it tends to stick even if you eventually loosen the reward. Yes, it's a bit reductive, training yourself like Pavlov's dog. But it beats the daily internal fight over screen time, for a kid or for yourself.

So I thought: what if the verification were real — an AI that actually checks whether a task got done, not just a checkbox — and the rewards were real too, pulled from an in-app marketplace? Say, 20 extra minutes of YouTube, unlocked through a small app talking to your router. Or, further out, a smart cabinet that pops open with a treat once you hit a bonus threshold for the day.

I started by trying to fork Habitica and bolt this on. Quickly realized it's easier to build from scratch. So GTD RPG was born — teams, boards, goals, quests, AI-verified completion, and a real-world marketplace behind it, built for kids, adults, teams, maybe even AI agents.

Here's the honest part: I got so deep into the architecture that I spent months just on specs. Zero product code, all design. Classic pet-project trap.

I'm digging back into it now. If you've wrestled with this — with screen time, with your own habits, or with getting lost in architecture before writing a line of product code — I'd love to hear how you got through it. And if gamified systems for real behavior change sound interesting to you, let's talk.
